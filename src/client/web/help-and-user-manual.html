Getting Started & How to Use

  1. First, read the README’s Overview section, and then, read the README’s Usage section.
  2. You must read and understand the Disclaimer and the Disclosure linked at the Disclaimer, before proceeding.
  3. For use with LP Ranger, your wallet must not have more than one single open (“open” meaning active, and meaning, “not closed”) liquidity position per liquidity pool. Otherwise, LP Ranger won’t be able to do the profit-and-loss, etc. reporting correctly. Positions in different pools are fine, and there is no limit on how many pools you use. Starting with no positions at all is also fine — you can create one and bring it under management afterwards.

    The reason for the limit is that LP Ranger traces each position’s profit and loss across its whole life, through every rebalance. With two positions open in the same pool, gains moving between them cannot be attributed to either one: the same tokens, in the same pool, in the same wallet, are indistinguishable on-chain. The same problem applies to residual coins — the leftovers that don’t fit the chosen liquidity range and stay in your wallet. With one position they clearly belong to it; with two there is no way to say whose they are, and any split would be a guess. Rather than report a number that looks precise but was guessed, LP Ranger asks for one position per pool.

    LP Ranger enforces this for itself: if you try to manage a position whose pool already has one under management, it declines and tells you which position holds that pool, so you can stop that one first if taking the pool over was what you wanted. It will not re-open a closed position while its pool holds an open one (see Closed Positions), and when it starts, each pool keeps one of the positions it was managing (see the FAQ on which positions it manages again after a restart). What it cannot do is stop a second position being created elsewhere. Opening another position in the same pool on 9mm directly, or adding liquidity to a drained NFT outside LP Ranger, puts two funded positions in that pool and brings back the very attribution problem the rule exists to avoid — LP Ranger has no way to refuse a transaction it did not send. Keep to one position per pool across every tool you use, not just inside LP Ranger.

    This limitation is also listed in the Prerequisites section of the README.
  4. If you are running on a Raspberry Pi 5 (recommended configuration: with Heat Sink and Fan, 5GB RAM, and Ethernet cable Internet connection instead of Wi-Fi) or similar device that defaults to a very low-resolution on the screen, consider setting the “Zoom” on your web browser to about 80%, to ease use of the app.
  5. If any liquidity position you want to use is more than 180 days old, you must get a Moralis API key and set it up in LP Ranger before it will work correctly for you. The free tier is sufficient. Click the gear icon (top right) → Settings to add your key, or enter it during wallet setup.

    The reason is that the only free and key-less source of historical prices for DeFi tokens, CoinGecko — which LP Ranger reaches through GeckoTerminal — serves only the past 180 days. Ask it for a price older than that and it refuses the request outright rather than answering. Moralis has no such limit, and it is the source LP Ranger prefers whenever a key is present.

    Without a key, any figure that needs a price from before that 180-day window cannot be worked out: the position’s entry value and HODL baseline, its original deposit value, and the historical columns of the Per-Day P&L table for periods that old. For a position opened within the last 180 days a key is still recommended for speed, but not required for correctness. See the Moralis questions in the FAQ below, including the recommendation to disable overage billing on your Moralis account.
  6. On first load of the app, wait for the initial synchronization of the app with the blockchain data to complete. This initial sync handles the default selected liquidity position, if any. It will be repeated for each brand-new liquidity pool that you have a position on. This is a read-only operation but can take a while. Don’t worry though: The results are cached so that, normally, you won’t have to wait again. Allow roughly an hour per position. A position that has rebalanced only a handful of times is ready well inside that; one with a hundred or more rebalances behind it takes somewhat longer. A valid Moralis API key that is not at its quota limit keeps the time down — without one, historical price lookups fall back to a slower, more heavily rate-limited source. Watch for the Sync Status badge near top-right on the app to read “Synced”. At that time, the blur on the app details section will clear.
  7. Next, consider which Liquidity Positions you want to manage, by clicking on the “Positions” button near center top on the app if in three column-view, or at the left if in single-column view. Alternatively, you can just manage the currently-loaded position. Before clicking the “Manage” button on any position, check that all settings on the app are appropriate for the gas cost on the blockchain you are using, and for the level of slippage that you will require.
  8. First, check the amount on the “Threshold” setting for Auto-compound. If gas costs are high, you will want this number to be large enough that gas costs are a small fraction of it. Next, scroll down to ensure that the settings under “Bot Configuration” are to your liking. For example, be sure to set a slippage percent that is appropriate for that pair (otherwise, the swaps to rebalance your LP position will fail due to insufficient slippage, or will be too costly if you set slippage too high). Also, if your pair requires high slippage, then you will want to control the frequency of rebalancing for that pair. There are several settings for this and the easiest is the per-day limit. For high-slippage, you might want to restrict rebalances to one per day, to control loss from slippage. Carefully read each circle-i tooltip for all the settings so that you understand them all. When you are ready, click the “Manage” button. Each managed position runs its own independent rebalance loop.
  9. Set Price Range Extension and Position Offset. In Bot Configuration under Range, turn the No Override toggle off so the badge reads Use Settings Below, then set the Price Range Extension percent to control how far the position spans from the current price. If you want an asymmetric token split, adjust the Position Offset; centred, 50/50 (the No Offset button), is right for most pairs. Leaving Price Range Extension blank keeps the position’s existing tick spread, placed around the price by the Position Offset (see “Price Range Extension, Range Width, and Position Offset” below for how the two width concepts differ). To force a full-range rebalance, use the Full-Range checkbox next to the Price Range Extension field. All persist per position and take effect on the next rebalance (manual or automatic). Click the circle-i info icon next to each field for the trade-off details.
  10. After you click Manage, wait for the “Sync” label near top right to read “Synced” again before relying on the figures. It takes as long as the first sync described above, and less when that position’s history is already cached.
  11. If you have your own PulseChain Archive RPC node, use it. The waits above are set by how fast LP Ranger is allowed to ask the blockchain questions. The free public endpoints permit only about five requests a second, so a first sync spends most of its time waiting its turn rather than working. If you have access to a PulseChain Archive node — a full archive, not a pruned one, since the sync reads state and event logs from years back that a pruned node cannot answer — and it allows far more traffic than that, make it your primary endpoint. Scroll down to Bot Configuration → Network, near the bottom of the app, and click Add RPC. A newly added endpoint goes to the top of the list, and the endpoint at the top is the one LP Ranger uses first. The shipped endpoints stay below it as fallbacks, so you lose nothing if your node goes down.

    One more step is needed to actually get the speed. LP Ranger paces itself, as shipped to a limit chosen for the free public endpoints, and it keeps to that pace no matter how fast your node is. Changing the endpoint alone will not speed anything up. The pace is set by globalRPCRequestRateIntervalMS, the minimum gap in milliseconds between any two requests. Lower it and LP Ranger asks faster: the current 222 is about 4.5 requests a second, 100 is ten, and 50 is twenty. It takes 5 to 2000.

    To change it, create the file app-config/user-configurable/bot-config-defaults.json if it is not already there, and put just the one setting in it:

    { "globalRPCRequestRateIntervalMS": 100 }

    Then restart LP Ranger. Do not edit the copy under app-config/app-defaults-for-user-configurable/ — that one ships with the app and is overwritten every time you update. The user-configurable copy is yours, is never overwritten by an update, and only needs the settings you actually want to change; everything you leave out keeps its shipped value. This setting is deliberately absent from the app’s settings screens, because on a public endpoint lowering the gap gets you rate-limited and makes the sync slower, not faster. Only lower it if the node is genuinely yours to saturate.
  12. When the price moves far enough out of range, or stays out long enough (the OOR threshold and the OOR timeout), the bot rebalances: it drains the old position and mints a new one around the current price. A rebalance lock ensures only one position transacts at a time (same wallet = same nonce).
  13. Each browser tab shows one position via URL. Open multiple tabs to monitor multiple pools simultaneously. If running on a lighter-weight machine such as a Raspberry Pi 5 (recommended configuration: with Heat Sink and Fan, 5GB RAM, and Ethernet cable Internet connection instead of Wi-Fi) or similar, it’s best to keep it to just one open web browser tab.
  14. Historical rebalance events are scanned up to 5 years back and used to calculate lifetime P&L.
  15. The $usd value of the total lifetime deposit is detected automatically, each deposit valued at the historical price of its own block. If a token’s price cannot be found, a dialog says so, and you can enter the price with Edit next to Current Value. To correct the deposit itself, use Edit Total Lifetime Deposit in the Lifetime panel, the right-hand card on a wide screen. An entry there always overrides the detected value.
  16. Wallet Residuals: Residuals are tokens that you intended to put into a liquidity position but that did not fit the chosen price range. They happen in two places: (1) when you first mint the position on the 9mm Pro dApp — the dApp deposits only what the range can accept at the current price and leaves the rest in your wallet on purpose; and (2) when the bot rebalances into a narrow new range and a little is left over. In both cases LP Ranger recognizes these wallet tokens as residuals for that specific pool, tracked separately from the on-chain LP value. On the very next rebalance the residuals are swept into the freshly minted position and become part of its value. They do not add to Total Lifetime Deposit, which counts only coins that reach the wallet from outside. If you would rather not wait, click “Rebalance Now” and confirm the IL warning; the residuals will be folded in immediately. If you want a wider range for that sweep, first turn off No Override in Bot Configuration → Range, raise the Price Range Extension (or check the Full-Range box for a full-range mint) and click Save. If multiple positions share a token, the wallet balance is split pro-rata based on each position’s in-range token amount.

FAQ

  • Q: Does LP Ranger charge any fees? A: LP Ranger does not charge any fees of its own, but the blockchain operations involved with providing liquidity do cost gas money.
  • Q: Can I lose money using LP Ranger? A: Generally, it’s the same risk as providing liquidity itself — so, yes, you could lose funds. But as well, although reasonable and commensurate precautions have been taken against this, LP Ranger itself could have bugs in it that could cause loss of funds. Don’t put in more funds than you can afford to lose.
  • Q: Is LP Ranger open-source? A: Yes, completely. See the License section of the README for details.
  • Q: Do I need to leave my computer on for LP Ranger to manage my positions? A: Yes. LP Ranger runs locally on your machine — if it isn’t running, no rebalancing, no compounding, and no notifications happen. LP Ranger runs well on a low-power always-on host like a Raspberry Pi 5 (recommended configuration: with Heat Sink and Fan, 5GB RAM, and Ethernet cable Internet connection instead of Wi-Fi).
  • Q: Is my private key safe? A: Your private key is encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM using a password you set, and is never sent off your machine. Only you can decrypt it. The plaintext key only exists in memory while the bot is running.
  • Q: Does LP Ranger support Uniswap V2-style positions? A: No. LP Ranger only manages V3 concentrated-liquidity NFT positions. V2 positions are rejected with a clear error.
  • Q: Can I manage multiple LP positions at the same time? A: Yes — LP Ranger is built for this, with this limitation. Each managed position runs its own independent rebalance loop, and a wallet-level lock serializes on-chain transactions so nonces stay clean.
  • Q: Do I need a Moralis API key? A: No, but it’s strongly recommended (free tier is sufficient). Without one, historical price lookups fall back to GeckoTerminal, which is rate-limited and can make the initial sync take significantly longer.
  • Q: If I sign up for a Moralis API key, will I get charged? A: Not if you cap your account at your current tier. Strongly recommended: after creating your Moralis account — whether you stay on the free tier or move to one of the various paid levels — open your Moralis account settings and disable overage / pay-as-you-go billing. Most paid API providers — Moralis included — enable overage charges by default, which means a usage spike that exceeds the quota on your current tier (free, or one of the various paid levels) can result in unexpected bills. With overage disabled, requests beyond your tier’s quota are refused rather than billed, and LP Ranger automatically falls back to GeckoTerminal — so capping the account costs you nothing in functionality. This same recommendation applies to any other optional third-party API key you add to LP Ranger: check each provider’s billing settings and turn overage off wherever it is offered.
  • Q: What happens if my computer crashes mid-rebalance? A: On restart, LP Ranger reloads all running positions from app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.json and resumes polling. It does not track a transaction sent before the crash: speed-up and auto-cancel cover only what the running server sends, and the bot’s new transactions wait behind one still pending. If one stays stuck, cancel or replace it from a wallet app that holds the same key.
  • Q: Which positions does LP Ranger manage again after it restarts? A: Every position you clicked Manage on and have not since stopped with Stop Managing. LP Ranger keeps that list, per wallet, in app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.json. Stopping LP Ranger, with npm run gentle-stop, npm run stop or Ctrl+C, leaves the list as it is. When LP Ranger starts again and the wallet is unlocked, it brings the wallet’s listed positions back under management one at a time, spread over one poll interval (5 minutes). Until it has dealt with every one, the Sync badge at top right reads “Syncing…”, which keeps Manage and Stop Managing disabled, and LP Ranger refuses to import a wallet. A position that can no longer be managed comes off the list: its NFT has left the wallet or no longer exists, or it shares its pool with another listed position. LP Ranger manages one active position per pool, so the pool keeps its open position, or the first listed if none is open, and the others come off the list. A position that failed to start stays on the list, as does one whose pool LP Ranger could not read from the chain, since it cannot check the rule without it; LP Ranger tries each again at the next start.
  • Q: Why does the dashboard sometimes show “WITHIN THRESHOLD” instead of rebalancing immediately when out of range? A: The price has left the range, but has not gone far enough past it, or stayed out long enough, to start a rebalance. That is the first gate under Rebalance, in the Gates section.
  • Q: Can I close LP Ranger and re-open it without losing data? A: Yes. Managed positions, settings and the encrypted wallet persist to disk in app-config/; the rebalance log, each position's records and the blockchain scan caches, the P&L history among them, in app-data/. Closing the browser tab is also fine — the server keeps running until you stop it.
  • Q: Does LP Ranger send any data to a server I don’t control? A: Only what’s needed to read on-chain data, fetch prices, route swaps and show token logos — RPC calls to your configured RPC endpoint, price/history calls to DexScreener, GeckoTerminal, or Moralis depending on your settings, and quote requests to the chain’s DEX aggregator (Watergun Aggregator) for routing rebalance swaps. Telegram notifications, if configured, go through Telegram’s API, and opening Check for Updates & About asks GitHub’s API for the latest release. To show token logos, your browser reads the chain’s token list as the dashboard loads. It then loads each logo from the address the list gives for it, so that site sees your ip address and which logo was asked for. Nothing else leaves your machine.
  • Q: What happens if the RPC provider goes down? A: LP Ranger ships with several RPC providers and moves down the list automatically when one stops responding. It sticks with the replacement for 1 hour, then tries the preferred one again. Any endpoint you added yourself sits at the top of that list, so it is tried first and the shipped ones remain behind it as backups.
  • Q: Can I point LP Ranger at my own RPC endpoint? A: Yes. In Bot Configuration under Network, click Add RPC, paste the URL, and click Save. What you add becomes the new primary and everything already listed drops one place to become failover — so running your own node does not cost you redundancy. The change applies immediately; there is no restart. Add a second endpoint later and it takes the top spot in turn.
  • Q: How do I switch to one of the other RPC endpoints? A: Open the RPC URL dropdown in Bot Configuration → Network. It lists every endpoint in the order the bot tries them, each marked PRIMARY or FAILOVER. The one showing is the primary, the one the bot uses unless it has failed over to the next. Pick another and it becomes the primary straight away, with the rest following behind it as failover. No restart.
  • Q: Why can’t I type an RPC URL straight into the dropdown? A: Because the endpoint is what every on-chain read and every transaction goes through, so pointing the bot somewhere new should take a deliberate act rather than a stray keystroke in a text field. Choosing from the dropdown is safe because every option is an endpoint that already exists; adding a new one goes through a dialog with Save and Close buttons, and nothing is committed until you press Save.
  • Q: I added an RPC endpoint by mistake, or one I added has gone bad. How do I remove it? A: Add a good endpoint — the new one becomes the primary and the bad one drops down the list. If the bad one never answers, the bot simply fails past it. To remove it outright, stop LP Ranger, open app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.json, and delete the entry from the rpcUrls list under global. That list holds the endpoints you added or picked from the dropdown; the shipped ones also live in chains.json and are always there as backups.
  • Q: Why didn’t the bot rebalance when I expected it to? A: A gate held it: one of the checks the bot makes before every automatic rebalance. The table under Rebalance, in the Gates section, lists each gate and where the dashboard shows which one is holding.
  • Q: Where’s my rebalance/compound? A: If you made it outside LP Ranger, on 9mm Pro or with another tool, see Rebalancing or Compounding Outside LP Ranger.
  • Q: What are the figures under a Rebalance or Compound entry in the Activity Log, and why do some read Old Log: Unavailable? A: See Figures under Rebalance and Compound entries and Missing figures, in the Activity Log section.
  • Q: Why do I see rebalances more frequently than my out-of-range time interval? A: Those extra rebalances are usually re-tries of an initial rebalance, not fresh out-of-range events. The common reason the bot fires a re-try shortly after an initial rebalance: the initial rebalance left a meaningful share of your funds as residual coins in your wallet — tokens that didn’t fit cleanly into the new range — so the bot waits 10 minutes and then fires an automatic residual cleanup rebalance to fold those leftovers back into the position (see Automatic Residual Cleanup below for the full criteria). A rebalance whose swap was refused because its price impact exceeded your Slippage setting is not retried, nor is one the price outran before its mint: each waits for you. Both are gates under Rebalance, in the Gates section. Every re-try passes the same gates as any automatic rebalance, the daily cap among them, so the total work stays bounded. This is intended behavior — the re-tries protect you from leaving funds idle in the wallet or from having the position drift while the price settles.
  • Q: My position is stuck syncing. A: The syncing can be halted from the LP Browser. Click the “Open Position(s)” button at the top right of the header to open the LP Browser. Select the stuck position, then click Remove at the bottom right of the LP Browser. The bot loop ends and the on-chain position is untouched. Re-scan the wallet using the “Scan Wallet” button at the top right of the LP Browser. To resume managing it, select the position again and click Manage.
  • Q: The Sync badge has said “Syncing…” far longer than usual. What is happening? A: Check the server log for a line containing Event scan error; it reads [bot] <pair> NFT #<id> …: Event scan error (<type>): …. That means the read of the position’s rebalance history failed, and the Lifetime figures are deliberately held back rather than computed from a history LP Ranger could not finish reading — a partial figure would look like a real one. The panels stay blurred while that is true, and the scan retries every 30 minutes on its own. Most read failures are a busy endpoint and clear themselves, because the app retries and moves to the next RPC. A failure caused by the request itself repeats instead, and the log line says which setting to change — for example, a rejected block range names the endpoint’s limit and tells you to lower getLogsChunkSize. If the log shows no such line, the position is simply still reading a long history; allow about an hour per position on the first pass.
  • Q: My compounded total $usd figure looks off. A: A price feed can occasionally serve a bad value — most often during heavy volatility, or on a pair whose two tokens share a symbol. Fees Compounded is kept as coin amounts and priced afresh at every poll, so a bad current price corrects itself at the next good one; if a token’s current price stays wrong, set it with Edit next to Current Value. To count the compounded coins again from the chain, use Re-scan Prices (see Fixing Bad Price Readings). If the figure is still wrong afterward, the cause is not a price, and Reload Current Position is the next step.
  • Q: What if I can’t find the answer in this FAQ? A: You may check (control-F / command-F (Mac)) the README, the Disclosure, the info at the circled “i” tooltips in the app user-interface, or the code itself. To search the code, sign in to GitHub, and type your search term in the search box at the GitHub Code Search.

Bookmarking Positions

The address bar updates as you switch positions. The URL looks like /pulsechain/<wallet>/<nft-contract>/<tokenId>. Add it to your browser bookmarks (or drag the address bar icon to the bookmarks bar) to jump straight to any position later. Opening a bookmark loads LP Ranger and activates that exact position as soon as the app is ready. Each bookmark is self-contained, so you can keep one per pool or one per NFT and switch between them with a single click. Bookmarks survive app restarts because they only reference on-chain identifiers — nothing in the URL depends on the server’s current managed-positions list.

All Positions Stats

LP Ranger lets you view the performance of all your LP positions at once, so you can see what’s working and what might need adjustment.

The All Stats button in the header (right of the Open Positions button) opens a sortable table across every currently-open managed position on your wallet. Columns: identity (blockchain, token pair, fee tier, LP provider), Lifetime P&L, Profit, Impermanent Loss/Gain, and a per-row Position link that takes you to that position’s dashboard tab.

Click any of the three numeric column headers to sort. First click sorts descending; a second click toggles ascending. The default sort is Lifetime P&L descending. Rows re-render on the same poll as the rest of the dashboard, every 3 seconds, so numbers stay live while the modal is open.

The button stays disabled until every currently-running managed position has finished its rebalance-history and lifetime-deposit scans. Hover the disabled button for a tooltip showing how many positions are still loading. For the meaning of each numeric column, click the “i” info icon on the Lifetime panel of any managed position; the same formulas power the modal.

Mission Control

Mission Control is the center card on the dashboard. It houses the manual action buttons and the auto-compound controls for the currently-viewed managed position.

  • Special Action badge at the top of the panel shows what the bot is doing on any managed position, and names that position: “Special Action: None”, Rebalancing (a residual cleanup shows as Rebalancing), or Compounding. If multiple actions overlap (rare), each stacks inside the badge.
  • Rebalance Now triggers a manual rebalance. Clicking it opens a short impermanent-loss confirmation dialog before firing. The rebalance uses the Price Range Extension, Full-Range checkbox, and Position Offset saved in Bot Configuration → Range for this position while that section’s No Override toggle is off; with it on, as every position starts, the existing range is re-used and re-centred on the price. There is no per-rebalance width prompt. A rebalance you start this way skips most gates, though not the gas check or a scan of the position under way: while LP Ranger reads the position’s history from the blockchain, as it does after every rebalance, the dialog shows why beside a spinner, and offers the rebalance again once the read ends. It counts toward the daily cap.
  • Compound Now collects any unclaimed fees and redeposits them into the current position. The button’s gates are under Compound, in the Gates section. No new NFT is minted; the same NFT gains liquidity. While LP Ranger reads the position’s history from the blockchain, a dialog opens instead, showing why beside a spinner, and offers the compound again once the read ends.
  • Auto-compound toggle: when on, the bot compounds automatically once unclaimed fees pass the Threshold you set below the toggle, in USD, as the compound gates allow. The toggle and the Threshold persist per position.

For deeper trade-offs on when to compound vs. rebalance, and on how the Threshold interacts with gas cost, click the “i” info icons next to Auto-compound and Threshold.

Activity Log

The Activity Log card lists what LP Ranger has done and what has happened in the app, newest first. Each entry has an icon, a title, a line of detail, and its time in UTC and in your local time. A Rebalance or Compound entry shows the start and end of its transaction’s hash; click the copy icon beside it to copy the whole hash. The line under an entry, where there is one, names its position: the pair, the LP provider, the NFT, the fee tier, the blockchain and the wallet.

  • What it records. The rebalances and compounds of your managed positions, and each stuck transaction LP Ranger cancelled (TX Auto-Cancelled). It also records what you do in the app: a wallet loaded or unlocked, a position loaded or viewed, a setting saved, an api key refused. A position that is not managed has no Rebalance or Compound entries.
  • What a fresh page shows. For each managed position, its latest compound, and its latest rebalance if it has rebalanced since LP Ranger last started. If the position on screen is managed, every rebalance and compound in its history, once the “Sync” label at top right reads “Synced”. Each managed position you bring on screen adds its own history the same way. New rebalances and compounds on any managed position appear as they happen.
  • How long entries stay. The log lives in the page, so a page reload starts it afresh, as described above. Entries for what you did in the app, such as a setting saved, do not come back. It keeps a set number of the newest entries, activityLogMaxEntries in ui-defaults_non-layout_for-server-up-scenario.json, and drops the oldest. Loading a different wallet, or clearing the wallet, empties it.
  • Rebalance entries read NFT #old → #new: the NFT the rebalance drained, and the one it minted. A rebalance also names what started it, (Out of Range), (Manual) or (Residual Cleanup), when LP Ranger has a record of it. The blockchain does not say, so a rebalance made outside LP Ranger names nothing, and so does an older one missing from LP Ranger’s rebalance log. If an older one in the log names nothing, Reload Current Position takes its label back from there.
  • Compound entries read NFT #id — $usd amount reinvested. A compound LP Ranger made is marked (Auto) or (Manual). One LP Ranger found on the blockchain instead, made before it managed the position or outside it, has no marker, since the blockchain does not say what started it. LP Ranger reads the blockchain for compounds when it first scans a position, and again on Reload Current Position and Re-scan Prices. A compound made outside LP Ranger in between appears after either. Historical and current prices, below, explains the prices its dollar amount uses, and the (historical $usd) mark after an amount at the prices of the time of the compound.

Figures under Rebalance and Compound entries

Under its date, each Rebalance and Compound entry shows a figure twice, as Current and Historical, explained below. For a Rebalance it is the Impermanent Loss/Gain of the rebalance’s swaps: what the coins it ended up with were worth against the coins it started with, as a share of the position it left, in whole percents. A minus sign means a loss. For a Compound it is the Percent Gain for Position: the fees it collected, as a share of the position right after it. That share is often well under 1%, so it shows to three decimal places. Coins you deposit are never counted as a gain: a rebalance sets aside a deposit still waiting in the wallet, and a compound counts only the fees it collected.

Historical and current prices

Historical is an entry’s figure at the prices of the moment its rebalance or compound happened, kept with the entry for good. Current is the same figure at today’s prices, kept up to date while the page is open. The $usd amount on a Compound entry is the coins the compound reinvested, at the prices LP Ranger had when it wrote the entry, and it does not change as prices move. An amount marked (historical $usd) is at the prices of the time of the compound: LP Ranger made the compound and wrote the entry then. An amount without the mark was written from the blockchain: when LP Ranger first scanned the position, or by Re-scan Prices or Reload Current Position. It is at the prices of the time that ran. Either button writes every Compound entry of the position on screen again, so the entries it rewrites lose their mark.

Missing figures

A dash means LP Ranger does not have the figure. A new rebalance’s Current fills in once the scan that follows it finishes. A figure the blockchain or the price services could not supply stays a dash.

An entry written before LP Ranger kept these figures reads Old Log: Unavailable. Two Settings buttons work its figures out from the blockchain: Reload Current Position for every Rebalance and Compound entry, and Re-scan Prices for the Compound entries only. Each entry shows its figures once the scan finishes. Each button works on the position on screen only, so another position’s entries keep Old Log: Unavailable until you bring that position on screen and run one there. Re-scan Prices or Reload Current Position? compares the two.

Fund Tracking

When a position is rebalanced, all collected tokens (principal + earned fees) are recycled into the new position. Any remainder that doesn't fit the new range stays in your wallet and is automatically counted toward your Lifetime P&L at current market prices, capped to your actual wallet balance. If you withdraw or sell tokens yourself, use Edit Realized Gains to account for them so your P&L stays accurate. Click the “i” info icon next to Realized Gains for the exact rules on how the field interacts with wallet residuals.

Rebalancing or Compounding Outside LP Ranger

LP Ranger records every rebalance and compound it makes as it happens, whether the bot started it or you clicked Rebalance Now or Compound Now. A rebalance or compound made outside LP Ranger, on 9mm Pro or with another tool, does not pass through LP Ranger, so LP Ranger does not record it. If you want to manage some liquidity yourself, do it with a different position, in a different wallet from the one LP Ranger uses. For a position LP Ranger manages, use the buttons in Mission Control instead.

Why a different wallet:

  • LP Ranger puts the wallet’s coins to work. A rebalance swaps and deposits the wallet’s whole balance of the pool’s two coins, not only what it took out of the old position, and a compound that swaps deposits the whole balance too. Coins you keep in that wallet for positions of your own end up in LP Ranger’s whenever they are one of a managed pool’s two coins, and a coin such as wPLS is in many pools.
  • LP Ranger counts every position in a pool as part of its own. It links each position the wallet opens in a pool into that pool’s chain of rebalances. That is why a wallet may hold only one open position per pool: a position of yours in a pool LP Ranger manages, in the same wallet, would be counted as one of LP Ranger’s.
  • A wallet’s transactions go out in a fixed order. Each one carries the next number in the wallet’s sequence, its nonce, and a rebalance or compound is several transactions in a row. A transaction you send from LP Ranger’s wallet while it is rebalancing or compounding (the Special Action badge in Mission Control says which) can take the nonce LP Ranger’s next transaction needs, and stop that rebalance or compound partway, with its coins left in the wallet.

If you do use LP Ranger’s wallet, LP Ranger’s figures leave out what you did until you reload the position. A rebalance you made has no row in the Rebalance Events table, and the fees a compound of yours reinvested are missing from the Lifetime panel’s Fees Compounded. Bring them in with Reload Current Position:

  1. Open the position LP Ranger manages in that pool. If you rebalanced that position yourself, LP Ranger keeps managing the drained one until about 30 minutes after it finds it empty, and never takes up the new one on its own. Until it stops, it holds the pool, so Manage on the new one is refused. Wait for it to stop, or click Stop Managing on the drained one; then select the new one and click Manage.
  2. Wait for the Sync badge at top right to read Synced.
  3. Click the Settings gear at top right, then Reload Current Position.

What the reload reads, and what it changes, is under Reload Current Position. Your own compounds appear in the Activity Log after the reload, with no (Auto) or (Manual) marker.

Automatic Residual Cleanup

After every successful rebalance, LP Ranger waits 10 minutes and then checks whether the wallet still holds a meaningful residual share of the position’s tokens. If the residual is still more than 5% of the combined LP + residual value for that pool, the bot fires one additional rebalance to sweep the leftover tokens into the position. This is called an automatic residual cleanup.

When it triggers. A cleanup arms itself only when every one of the following is true:

  • At least 10 minutes has passed since the last rebalance.
  • The residual wallet share exceeds 5% of (LP + residual) for the pool.
  • No rebalance is currently in progress, paused, or already requested by you.
  • A cleanup has not already been consumed since the last normal rebalance (see “Back-to-back protection” below).

Back-to-back protection. A cleanup may fire at most once per normal rebalance. If the cleanup itself does not reduce the residual below 5% — for example, because liquidity at the edge of the range is too thin — the bot does not keep trying. The cleanup opportunity is reset only when the next out-of-range or manual rebalance succeeds. This prevents the bot from oscillating on pools where a small residual is structurally unavoidable.

How the sweep decides it’s done. The cleanup rebalance runs the same execution as any other rebalance: remove liquidity, swap, corrective-swap loop, mint. The corrective-swap loop uses LP Ranger’s inflation-resistant dust threshold as its exit condition. That threshold is gold-pegged (via a PAXG / XAUT reference in dust-threshold.json) instead of USD-pegged so it does not creep up with fiat inflation. The 5% pool-share is only what arms the cleanup; the actual sweep continues until residual imbalance falls below the dust threshold or the corrective-swap iteration cap is reached.

All the usual gates still apply. The cleanup is simply a reason to ask for a rebalance: it passes every gate an automatic rebalance does, except that it can run while the price is in range. And when a cleanup does run, it counts toward the daily cap like any other rebalance.

What you’ll see. While a cleanup rebalance is in flight, the range banner at the top of the position card turns yellow and flashes, reading “Rebalancing to Reduce Residual Wallet Coin Amount”. The resulting entry in the Activity Log is tagged “(Residual Cleanup)” so you can distinguish it from an out-of-range or manual rebalance at a glance. There is no separate Telegram alert — cleanup rebalances use the same “Rebalance Succeeded / Failed” channels as any other rebalance.

If you don’t want to wait. You can always click Rebalance Now to sweep residuals immediately. Confirm the IL warning that appears and the rebalance fires with the range the No Override toggle selects: the existing one re-centred, or your saved Range settings. If you want a wider range for that rebalance, turn No Override off, raise the Price Range Extension (or check Full-Range), click Save, then click Rebalance Now.

Tuning. The delay (currently 10 minutes) and threshold (currently 5%) are set in app-config/app-defaults-for-user-configurable/bot-config-defaults.json under the residualCleanup group (fields delayMs and thresholdPct). They are intentionally not exposed in the Bot Configuration UI — they are cross-cutting defaults that apply to every managed position. To override, copy the file app-config/app-defaults-for-user-configurable/bot-config-defaults.json to app-config/user-configurable/bot-config-defaults.json, and edit your copy to set just the residualCleanup keys you want to change (you do not need to copy the rest — the runtime deep-merges your copy on top of the shipped defaults, so any key you omit keeps its shipped value). Your copy in user-configurable/ survives tarball upgrades; the original in app-defaults-for-user-configurable/ must stay untouched.

Price Range Extension, Range Width, and Position Offset

These live in Bot Configuration under Range, persist per position, and, while the section’s No Override toggle is off, take effect on the next rebalance of any kind — automatic out-of-range, OOR-timeout, automatic residual cleanup, manual Rebalance Now, and closed-position re-opens all mint their new range from these settings. None of them is a gate — they never block or delay a rebalance; they only shape the minted range. No forced rebalance is needed to change any of them.

Price Range Extension vs Range Width — two different concepts

LP Ranger distinguishes two related but different concepts for “how wide is the position”. Only the first is implemented today; the second is a future feature on the road map.

  • Price Range Extension (implemented): how far the position’s price range extends from the current price, expressed as a percentage of the current price. At a centered 50/50 offset, a Price Range Extension of 50 means the position covers 25% below current price and 25% above current price — so from 0.75× current to 1.25× current. A value of 10 covers ±5% around current (0.95× to 1.05×).
  • Range Width (future / on the road map, see the “Nice to Have’s” list): the percentage of the pool’s currently-populated liquidity range that the position covers. This is the LP-native concept — “how much of where liquidity actually exists am I in?”. It is more useful for deciding where to place coins because it accounts for the pool’s real liquidity distribution rather than an abstract percentage-of-current-price. It is not yet implemented; the app documents it here so the terminology is consistent with future work.

Different LP creators (for example the 9mm dApp’s own position UI) may label their width slider using yet a third convention — linear-vs-exponential math, symmetric-in-price vs symmetric-in-tick, and so on. A position you set up as “50%” on 9mm may not read as “50” in LP Ranger’s Price Range Extension field, because the two systems use different denominators. That is expected; LP Ranger is self-consistent internally.

What a position does before you set anything

Every position starts with the No Override toggle on and the badge reading Re-Use Existing Position Range, so out of the box the bot keeps the position’s existing width on every rebalance: its on-chain tick spread is carried across the rebalance and re-centred on the current price, subject only to standard liquidity-pool tick rounding, which can drift the spread by up to one tick-spacing per rebalance. Nothing you have not explicitly turned on and saved will reshape your position. See The No Override toggle below.

Inside Use Settings Below mode, a Price Range Extension field left empty also keeps the existing tick spread, but the Position Offset places it around the current price: centred only at an offset of 50. The input stays blank until you type a value, so a blank field never looks like a saved one. The Default button fills in the default if you want a starting point.

Full-Range checkbox

To the right of the Price Range Extension field is a Full-Range checkbox. When checked, every rebalance mints a full-range position (from the pool’s minimum tick to its maximum tick — effectively “price zero to infinity”), and the Price Range Extension value is ignored. The field is disabled while the box is checked. If the currently-active position is already full-range on-chain, the box shows as checked automatically, reflecting on-chain reality, even without an explicit save. If your own copy of bot-config-defaults.json sets fullRangeRebalanceEnabled to true, a position with no choice saved shows the box checked, and its rebalances mint full-range while its Range settings apply.

Edit-row buttons

  • Default: fills the input with the default from bot-config-defaults.json, or your override of it, and unticks Full-Range — asking for a default extension is asking not to mint full-range, and the two cannot both apply. You still have to click Save to persist either change.
  • Save: writes the extension and the Full-Range setting to the per-position config together; they apply on the next rebalance.

There is no per-field clear. The Price Range Extension is either disabled or in force, and the No Override toggle described below is what decides which.

The No Override toggle

The Range section is headed by a single toggle labelled No Override, with a status badge beside it. The toggle governs every setting in the section together: Price Range Extension, its Full-Range checkbox, and Position Offset.

  • Toggle on — badge reads Re-Use Existing Position Range. None of the three is applied. The bot carries the position’s current on-chain price range across the rebalance and re-centres it on the price at that moment, so the new position is as wide as the one it replaces. The fields are disabled. This is where every position starts out.
  • Toggle off — badge reads Use Settings Below. The fields are enabled and their saved values shape every subsequent rebalance.

Switching back and forth is safe. Turning the toggle on does not erase anything: your saved Price Range Extension, Full-Range and Position Offset values stay exactly as you left them, greyed out and ignored. Turn it off again and they are back in force unchanged.

The setting is saved per position, so each pool can be in a different mode.

Upgrading from an earlier version? A position that already had a Price Range Extension, a ticked Full-Range box, or a Position Offset saved against it opens in Use Settings Below, not on the default — it keeps applying exactly what it applied before, so an upgrade never quietly changes how a running position rebalances. Positions with none of those saved open on Re-Use Existing Position Range, which is what they were already doing.

Impermanent Loss Guard

What it does

Before every automatic rebalance, the bot works out what the new position would be worth and compares it against what simply holding the coins this NFT was minted with would be worth at today’s prices. The gap between the two is the position’s impermanent loss, the IL/G the Current panel shows, and a rebalance would lock it in. If the new position would come out more than this percentage below holding, the rebalance is rejected and the position is left exactly as it is. Nothing is withdrawn, collected, or swapped — the check is a calculation, not a trial run.
If a rebalance empties the old position and then fails to mint the new one, the Guard stands aside while the bot tries the mint again: the loss is locked in by then, and turning the mint down would only leave the coins idle in the wallet.

Which value it measures against

The coins the current NFT was minted with, valued at today’s prices. Not what they were worth on the day of the mint: a move both coins make together changes that figure, and a rebalance does not lock that move in. Not your lifetime deposit into the pool either, and not any figure spanning earlier NFTs in the rebalance chain. Each time a rebalance does go through, the new NFT’s own coins become what the Impermanent Loss Guard (ILG) measures against. The comparison is even: the same coins, as they were minted and as the position holds them now. Coins in the wallet count on neither side, whether a rebalance’s leftovers or a deposit waiting there. Coins a compound takes from the wallet into the NFT count with the minted ones, since the position now holds them. Today’s prices are those the bot reads at that check; while price lookups are paused, as they are while the dashboard sits idle, it reads fresh prices before it measures.

Worked example

Holding the coins this NFT was minted with would be worth $1,000 at today’s prices, and the Impermanent Loss Guard (ILG) is set to 15% (the default). Rebalancing stops if the position would come out below $850. At $910 it rebalances normally; at $820 it is rejected and left alone.

Retries back off, 4 hours to 1 week

After a rejection the position is left alone rather than re-decided on every poll. The first retry comes 4 hours later, and each further rejection doubles the wait — 4h, 8h, 16h, 32h — up to a ceiling of 1 week. The moment the Guard lets a rebalance through, the ladder resets to 4 hours.
This bounds how often a rejected rebalance is retried, and the Telegram alert follows the same schedule, so a position blocked for a long stretch reports on a widening interval instead of every poll. The cost is that a position which recovers inside a window waits for it to expire. Rebalance Now is never held by the wait.

What lifts a rejection

A position rejected by the Impermanent Loss Guard cannot rebalance, so it cannot mint a new NFT, so it goes on measuring against the same minted coins. The bot looks again when the retry wait runs out, or at its next check once you save a new Guard percentage here. The block lifts at that check if the position’s value is back above the floor, or if the new percentage brings the floor below that value. Rebalance Now ignores the Guard, though like any rebalance it waits while its gas would cost more than 0.5% of the position’s value.

Choosing a value

The two sides of this trade are not alike, which is why the default is tight. Rebalancing crystallizes the loss: the position is re-minted around today’s price, so a paper loss that a price recovery would have undone becomes permanent. Declining costs you fees — but the check only comes up when a rebalance was due, which usually means the position is already out of range and earning few fees anyway. And fees are what would have to earn that loss back. In a quiet pool that can take longer than you hold the position.
15% (the default) leaves ordinary impermanent loss to rebalance and holds back when it runs deep.
Higher rebalances through steeper losses, so the position keeps earning — at the price of locking those losses in.
Lower protects harder, and will stop rebalancing often on a swingy pair.
100% effectively turns it off.

When volume is very low

Very low trading volume pays the position little in fees, too little to pay for each rebalance or to earn back the loss a rebalance locks in. Then set the Impermanent Loss Guard very low, for example 5%, and limit Max Rebalances Per Day to 1.

What is not included

Swap slippage and gas are not part of this figure. A rebalance moves the same dollars from one price range to another, so the projection is the position’s present value — its liquidity, without the coins in the wallet. Fees compounded into this NFT are left out, as they are in IL/G: they are earnings, and counting them would hide a loss.

Impermanent gains never block

A position worth more than holding is above the floor by definition, whatever the Guard is set to, so a gain can never reject a rebalance.

Telegram

The Rebalance Rejected Due to Excessive Impermanent Loss alert is on by default; without it, a rejection shows only in the dashboard’s “Rebalance skipped — impermanent loss” dialog and in the server log. It carries the figures, says the position was not touched, and links to the User Manual for this explanation rather than repeating it. It follows the retry schedule above, so you get one message per retry, not one per poll.

Position Offset

The Position Offset controls how the Price Range Extension is split above versus below current price. At 50/50 (the No Offset button), the range is centred on the current price. At 40/60 or other skewed values, more of the range sits on one side, giving you a more asymmetric token composition on rebalance.

The No Offset button fills both fields with the centered 50/50 split; like the Default button on the Price Range Extension row, it does not persist anything on its own. Click Save to write it.

For the full trade-off explanation (fee capture vs. impermanent loss, out-of-range behaviour, etc.), click the “i” info icon next to each field in the app.

Slippage Tolerance (per-token)

Slippage tolerance is set independently for each token in the pair. This handles asymmetric-liquidity pairs cleanly: one side may be thinly traded and need a high slippage to complete swaps, while the other side is deep and needs a low value to avoid opening its swaps up to MEV front-running. A single slippage value could not do both jobs at once; per-token slippage does.

Both inputs live in Bot Configuration → Execution, labelled Slippage When Swapping to Token 0 (<token 0 name>) and Slippage When Swapping to Token 1 (<token 1 name>). Token names update automatically to match the active pool.

Default value

Both inputs start populated with the default (0.75%). That is the value the bot uses on that side until you change it. Type a different value in either input and click Save to persist it for this position. On upgrade from a version that only had one Slippage Tolerance input, the previously saved value is not migrated — both per-token inputs start at the default. You can re-tune them as needed.

Which side applies to which swap

The bot uses the destination-token rule: a swap that CONVERTS Token 0 INTO Token 1 uses the Slippage When Swapping to Token 1 value, and a swap that CONVERTS Token 1 INTO Token 0 uses the Slippage When Swapping to Token 0 value. The destination side is where MEV can extract value from the swap, so that’s where the slippage budget lives.

Allowed range and warnings

The full allowed range per side is 0.1% to 20%. When you Save a value above 5%, the app opens a confirm dialog before writing it. When you Save a value at 10% or higher, the app opens a stricter dialog that requires you to type Confirm (case-sensitive) into a text box before it proceeds. Values outside 0.1% to 20% are rejected and the app shows a warning stating the valid range. On asymmetric pairs, the deep side typically wants 0.1% to 1% and the thin side may need 5% or more.

Gates

A gate is a check the bot makes before it compounds or rebalances a position on its own. While a gate holds, the bot leaves the position as it is and asks again at its next poll, every 5 minutes. Gates keep automatic moves from costing more than they earn: paying gas for every wick of the price, rebalancing again and again while the market swings, or locking in a loss that a recovery would have undone. A move you start yourself, with Compound Now or Rebalance Now, skips most of them.

Volatility Doubling

If three rebalances occur within 4× the minimum interval (e.g. 40 minutes when the minimum is 10 minutes), the bot enters doubling mode. Each subsequent rebalance must wait twice as long as the previous one: 10m → 20m → 40m → 80m, and so on. This prevents rapid-fire rebalancing during high volatility. Doubling mode clears automatically if no rebalance is needed for 4× the current wait, or at the daily midnight UTC reset.

All successful rebalances count toward the three in-window triggers — automatic out-of-range, OOR-timeout, residual cleanups, and manual Rebalance Now clicks alike. Once doubling mode is active, the doubled wait blocks only the automatic rebalances; manual clicks are never blocked, but each successful rebalance while doubling is active — manual included — doubles the wait again.

Max Rebalances / Day (the Daily Cap)

Max Rebalances / Day (in Bot Configuration) is a per-pool daily safety cap. Every successful rebalance counts toward it, no matter what triggered it: automatic out-of-range rebalances, out-of-range timeout rebalances, automatic residual cleanup follow-on rebalances, and manual Rebalance Now clicks. Compounding is a separate operation and never counts.

Once a pool reaches its cap, the throttle badge shows CAPPED and no automatic rebalance runs for that pool until the counter resets at midnight UTC (or you raise the setting). Two clarifications that matter in practice:

  • Manual clicks are never blocked — but they still count. You can always click Rebalance Now, even while CAPPED; you stay in control of your own position. Each click adds one to the day’s count, though, so automatic rebalances (including residual cleanups) remain blocked until the midnight UTC reset.
  • The cap is per pool, not global. Each pool’s daily count is independent, so a volatile pool hitting its cap does not stop rebalances on your other pools. Counts survive an app restart — they are re-seeded from the local rebalance log (app-data/rebalance_log.json).

Which Rebalances Each Setting Holds

Rebalances come from four triggers: automatic out-of-range (price crossed the OOR threshold), OOR-timeout (price stayed out of range past the time threshold), automatic residual cleanup (follow-on sweep of wallet leftovers), and manual (you clicked Rebalance Now, including a closed-position re-open). Here is which of them three Rebalance Timing settings hold, and which they do not. Volatility doubling and the daily cap are in their own subsections, above.

  • Min Time Between Rebalances — blocks all three automatic triggers (out-of-range, OOR-timeout, residual cleanup) until the cooldown elapses. Never blocks a manual click. However, every successful rebalance of any kind — manual and cleanup included — restarts the cooldown clock, so a manual rebalance delays the next automatic one by this interval.
  • OOR Threshold Before Rebalance Is Triggered (±%) — consulted only by the automatic out-of-range trigger. Residual-cleanup and manual rebalances ignore it entirely (they can run even while the price is inside the range). The OOR-timeout trigger fires despite it.
  • OOR Rebalance Time Threshold — a second trigger for automatic rebalances only; residual-cleanup and manual rebalances do not consult it. The countdown starts at the first out-of-range poll and resets when the price returns to range or when any rebalance succeeds. A timeout-triggered rebalance still passes every later gate — the timeout is a trigger, not a bypass.

Gas Cost Protection

Before each rebalance, the bot estimates gas cost in USD. If gas would exceed 0.5% of the LP position value, the rebalance is deferred and the bot looks again every 1 hour instead of at its usual poll interval (5 minutes). Normal polling resumes with the first look that no longer has to wait for gas. The bot always starts with the normal polling interval. This gate holds Rebalance Now too: a rebalance you start waits for gas the same way.

Closed Positions

When you select a closed position (zero liquidity), the bot will display its data but will not attempt to rebalance it. If you re-open a previously closed position by adding liquidity back to the same NFT on 9mm Pro, the bot treats it as brand new—duration, fees, and P&L all start from the original mint date. Recommendation: instead of re-opening a closed position, mint a fresh LP position on 9mm Pro. This gives you a clean start with accurate tracking and avoids any ambiguity in your P&L history.

Within LP Ranger, a closed position can only be re-opened when its pool has no open position at all, and then only if it is the newest closed one. Re-opening is there to recover a position that a rebalance drained and then failed to replace, and that is always the newest one. On any older closed position the Manage button is disabled and its tooltip names the newer one. You can still select an older closed position and view its full history — only the re-open is refused. The LP Position Browser lists positions newest first, so the re-openable one is the first of a pair’s closed rows. If the pool still holds an open position, nothing there needs recovering — manage that position instead, since re-opening a closed one beside it would put two funded positions in the same pool. If a re-open fails and you click Manage again to retry while LP Ranger is reading the position’s history from the blockchain, it declines and asks you to click Manage again once the read finishes.

Telegram Notifications

LP Ranger can send real-time alerts to your phone via Telegram. You choose which events trigger a notification:

  • OOR Timeout Triggered (default ON) — the position has been continuously out of range for longer than the configured timeout, so the bot will now try to rebalance. A gate can still hold it. While one does, the alert comes again at most once a day (UTC).
  • Rebalance Succeeded (default OFF) — a rebalance completed successfully and a new NFT was minted.
  • Rebalance Rejected Due to Excessive Impermanent Loss (default ON) — the Impermanent Loss Guard blocked a rebalance because the position’s impermanent loss, measured against holding the coins its NFT was minted with at today’s prices, is deeper than the Guard allows. The message carries the figures, confirms the position was not touched, and links back to this manual for the full explanation. Without it, a rejection shows only in the dashboard’s “Rebalance skipped — impermanent loss” dialog, while a dashboard is open, and in the server log.
  • Rebalance Failed (default ON) — a rebalance attempt failed (on-chain revert, RPC error, etc.), or stopped before its mint because the price moved out of the new range; then the message asks you to weigh any impermanent loss and, if it is acceptable, to click Rebalance Now.
  • Compound Succeeded (default OFF) — unclaimed fees were successfully compounded back into the position.
  • Compound Failed (default ON) — a compound attempt failed.
  • Other Error (default ON) — an unexpected error occurred during a poll cycle, including stuck transactions that were auto-cancelled. Does not include server/bot shutdown, which has its own event below.
  • Low Gas Balance (default ON) — the wallet’s native token balance (e.g. PLS) has dropped into the amber “top up soon” zone: below the recommended cushion (one worst-case rebalance × 3× safety × number of managed positions) but still above the one-rebalance floor. The alert fires once per low-balance episode and resets when the balance recovers above the recommended threshold, so you won’t be spammed.
  • Very Low Gas (default ON) — the wallet’s native token balance has dropped below the one-rebalance floor and the Mission Control gas badge is flashing red. The next rebalance will fail on gas. This alert fires once when the tier drops to critical and resets only when the balance recovers to the “ok” tier.
  • Server and Bot Shutdown/Exit (default ON) — the server (which includes the bot) is shutting down: when npm run gentle-stop asks it to stop, or on Ctrl+C, npm run stop or any other SIGINT or SIGTERM signal. A manual restart may be required.

Setup:

  1. Open Telegram and message @BotFather. Send /newbot, follow the prompts, and it will give you a bot token (looks like 123456789:ABCdefGhI...).
  2. Save the bot token to a safe place. You will need to copy it soon.
  3. Start a chat with your new bot and send any message (just type something and press Enter).
  4. In your web browser, go to: https://api.telegram.org/bot<your-bot-token>/getUpdates (replace <your-bot-token> with the token from Step 2, no brackets).
  5. In the JSON response, find your chat ID at result[0].message.chat.id — it is a numeric value near the top of the response, inside the first result entry, under message → chat → id. Copy this number.
  6. In LP Ranger, open the Telegram setup dialog from the Settings gear menu or from the wallet setup screen.
  7. Paste your bot token and chat ID, select the events you want, and click Save.
  8. Click Test to verify — you should receive a test message in Telegram.

Your bot token is encrypted on disk with the same wallet password used for your private key. It is never sent anywhere except the Telegram Bot API.

Fixing Bad Price Readings

Every dollar figure LP Ranger shows is an on-chain amount times a token price. The amounts come from the blockchain and are reliable. The prices come from outside price services, and one can occasionally answer with a bad value, which then sticks in any figure stored in dollars. Two buttons in Settings fix that. They also rebuild LP Ranger’s cached records of the position, so older records gain annotations added since, such as the figures in the Activity Log: Re-scan Prices for compounds, Reload Current Position for compounds and rebalances.

Re-scan Prices or Reload Current Position?

Both work on the position on screen, only while it is managed and Synced, and not while it is rebalancing or compounding. Neither touches your position, your funds, or its on-chain history.

Re-scan Prices

In Settings, click Re-scan Prices. It re-values the position’s stored dollar figures at freshly fetched prices: Total Lifetime Deposit, and the LP entry value detected for the position you are viewing: its value when the current NFT position was created. That is the point in time from which the Impermanent Loss Guard measures impermanent loss. It also re-reads the compounded coin amounts behind Fees Compounded from the chain; those are priced afresh at every poll anyway.

With them it rebuilds the position’s Compound entries in the Activity Log, as that section describes. It leaves the Rebalance entries as they are; Reload Current Position works out their figures.

Each figure is replaced only once its new value arrives, so a re-scan that is interrupted, or one whose price feeds stay silent, leaves your existing figures as they were. It re-reads the position’s own history from the blockchain, which can take minutes with a Moralis API key, and usually much longer, into hours, with only the free price services. It skips the pool history that Reload Current Position re-scans, so it finishes sooner.

The dialog also offers two options, both off by default. The first is Also re-price the Per-Day P&L table. Each row of that table is a closed accounting period, and a period keeps its dollar figures but not the token amounts behind them — so re-pricing one means reading its NFT’s history again, which takes minutes on a long rebalance chain. Leave it unticked unless the gas or the daily figures in that table are what look wrong; the rows stay on screen and are replaced only as their new values arrive.

The second, Limit to the last N days, becomes available once the first is ticked, and is the quick way to fix one bad recent price: only the periods that closed inside the window are re-read, and everything older keeps the figures it already holds, so the repair takes a moment rather than minutes. It is offered only alongside the Per-Day option because nothing else this button re-values is tied to a date — the other figures are whole-position totals that need every NFT in the chain. If the table is not yet complete, the window is ignored and the whole chain is rebuilt, because a stored period does not record which NFT it came from, so a partial rebuild could not tell a missing period from an old one.

If the button in its dialog is greyed out, the position is not managed: click Manage first.

Re-scan Prices and Reload Current Position are not available while a rebalance or compound of the position is under way: from the moment LP Ranger starts deciding it, including any wait for another position’s move, until it is done, and while a Rebalance Now or Compound Now you clicked waits to be made. LP Ranger then answers “A coin move (rebalance or compound) for this position has already started. Try again once it’s complete.”

Reload Current Position

In Settings, click Reload Current Position. It rebuilds every on-chain-derived figure for the position (compound history, HODL baseline, deposits, cached epochs, cached event log) by re-running the initial scan from the pool’s creation.

That scan reads the wallet’s history in the pool again from the blockchain: every position opened in the pool, and every deposit, fee collection and withdrawal on each. It links each rebalance made outside LP Ranger into the chain, as if LP Ranger had made it, and counts the fees such compounds reinvested. One reload therefore catches every rebalance and compound made on that wallet in that pool, by LP Ranger or by you, except on a position that was burned rather than kept: burning erases which pool a position was in. LP Ranger never burns the positions it drains.

Afterward, the Routed Via column of the Rebalance Events table shows a dash for LP Ranger’s earlier rebalances. LP Ranger records the route a swap took only as it makes the rebalance; it does not yet read routes back from the blockchain.

The Activity Log’s Rebalance and Compound entries are rebuilt with it, each with its figures. Whether each compound LP Ranger made was Auto or Manual, and the figures it recorded at the time, are kept. A rebalance gets back what started it only where LP Ranger’s rebalance log, app-data/rebalance_log.json, holds it, since the blockchain does not record that.

Use it when Lifetime figures look wrong for a specific position (fees compounded, IL/G, deposit total), after a rebalance or compound made outside LP Ranger (see Rebalancing or Compounding Outside LP Ranger), or when a red “Catastrophic scan failure” dialog surfaces (see Where do error details go?).

It is not available while a rebalance or compound of the position is under way, as for Re-scan Prices.

While the scan runs, LP Ranger shows a full-screen blocking dialog and disables the rest of the UI; when the scan finishes, the page automatically reloads. With a Moralis API key, allow about an hour, longer for a position with many rebalances behind it or a slow RPC endpoint. With only the free price services it usually takes much longer, into hours. Other managed positions are untouched.

Troubleshooting

  • Check the Settings First: Run the validate-config-values-with-any-user-overrides diagnostic.
  • Check Sync Status: Does the “Sync” status at top right show “Synced”? If not, wait for it. Data and controls may be incomplete until the initial blockchain scan finishes.
  • Every Figure Shows a Dash and No Button Works: Right after you switch to another position, LP Ranger shows a dash in place of every figure and disables every button until the server has answered about that position, so nothing on screen can be the previous position’s. This normally lasts a moment. If it lasts, the server is not answering: the status at top right reads HALTED once several questions in a row go unanswered. Check that the server is running; everything comes back as soon as it answers. If the wallet is locked, unlock it, which stays possible throughout.
  • Rebalance Not Completing: If the blockchain is busy, a rebalance could take longer than expected. The progress of a rebalance can be seen on the Activity Log of this app, where the creation of the new NFT will be seen logged as a distinct log item. Also, it can be monitored by viewing the server console log.
  • Stuck Transaction Auto-Cancel: If a rebalance transaction does not confirm within 2 minutes, the bot automatically resends it at a higher gas price. If the transaction is still stuck after 1 hour total, the bot cancels it by sending a zero-value self-transfer at the same nonce, freeing the wallet for future transactions. The speed-up is reported on the server console; a cancel is also shown in the Activity Log as TX Auto-Cancelled. The aggregator swap is the exception: it waits its own limit, set per blockchain in chains.json, then cancels and asks for a fresh quote. These timeouts are configurable, in seconds, via TX_SPEEDUP_SEC (currently 120) and TX_CANCEL_SEC (currently 3600) in the .env file.
  • Persistent Transaction Problems: If transactions continue to fail or get stuck, try adding a different RPC endpoint with the Add RPC button in the Network section toward the bottom of the app under Bot Configuration. What you add becomes the endpoint the bot uses, immediately.
  • Price Discovery Failures: If USD price lookups are failing, add a free Moralis API key in Settings → Moralis API Key. Current prices are asked of Moralis when a key is stored, then GeckoTerminal, then DexScreener. See USD Pricing in docs/engineering.md for details.
  • Prices Don’t Look Right: Try adding your Moralis API key (free) so that Moralis can be a data source. Moralis is a remotely-hosted crypto data API that provides token prices, historical block-level pricing, and other on-chain data. It is the most reliable price source for tokens that other free services miss. Click the gear icon (top right) to add your key in Settings. If one token’s current price stays wrong, set it yourself with Edit next to Current Value.
  • Slow Initial Scans: The first blockchain scan fetches historical prices for every rebalance epoch. Adding a Moralis API key (free) makes this much faster by bypassing GeckoTerminal rate limits. Click the gear icon (top right) → Settings.
  • Lost Managed Positions or Settings: On every startup the app saves a backup of your configuration to app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.backup.json. If your managed positions or settings are lost, copy the backup over the main file: cp app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.backup.json app-config/user-configurable/bot-config.json and restart. See The app-config Directory in docs/engineering.md for the full layout.
  • Current or Lifetime Panel Not Populating: If the Current and/or Lifetime panels are missing data or look stale, re-select the position you want to view from the LP Browser. This triggers a fresh fetch and redraw for that position.
  • Position Stuck on “Syncing…”: If the sync badge at top right stays on “Syncing…” for longer than expected (allow about an hour per position, occasionally more on very old pools and under slow external conditions) and appears wedged — no progress in the Activity Log, no new entries in the server console — a passing network problem may have cut the page off from the scan. First try a browser page reload: the page reconnects, and the scan still running on the server can then report its result. If the badge is still stuck after the reload, click the Settings gear at top right, then click Reload Current Position. See Reload Current Position for what that button does and how long it takes.
  • Where do error details go? When a catastrophic scan failure occurs, LP Ranger writes the full stacktrace and timestamp to logs/error.log in your installation directory (same folder as the diagnostic lp-ranger.log). To move it, put { "errorLogPath": "<path>" }, relative to the installation directory, in app-config/user-configurable/logging.json and restart. Each incident is separated from the previous one by four blank lines. The file is emptied each time LP Ranger starts, so copy it before restarting if you mean to report a bug. This file is reserved for catastrophic failures only; routine warnings and info are not written here. Include the relevant section when reporting a bug.
  • Web browser seems stuck while trying to load a new Liquidity Position: If it’s been over an hour, then click the Web Browser’s reload button. This is necessary if the Web Browser’s Console Log shows a “403 Forbidden” error(s). No need for a hard refresh; just click the refresh button.
  • The $usd totals for my LP Position in Unmanaged don’t seem to match up with those shown in the Managed state: Both views value the Current panel’s compounded fees and gas at today’s prices, so their figures should agree once both have finished loading; prices that moved between the two looks account for small differences. Only the Managed view shows the Current panel’s Profit, the Lifetime panel and the Per-Day P&L table. Profit needs LP Ranger’s own record of each compound, which it keeps only while it manages the position. To see them, click Manage.
  • Turning Sounds Off: LP Ranger plays short audio cues on successful rebalance, successful compound, and when you click “Manage Position.” To silence them, click the Settings gear icon at top right and uncheck Sounds. The choice is saved in your browser and survives restarts. Re-check the box to turn sounds back on. Sounds default to on for a fresh browser profile. To flip the operator-configured default to off for any user who clears their browser storage or visits for the first time, put { "soundsEnabled": false } in app-config/user-configurable/ui-defaults_non-layout_for-server-up-scenario.json and restart LP Ranger. Your copy in user-configurable/ survives tarball upgrades; the original in app-defaults-for-user-configurable/ must stay untouched.
  • Why didn’t all my fees get compounded on the latest compound? A compound runs a small ratio-correcting swap before the deposit, so in most cases nearly all of the fees end up in the position. The swap is skipped, however, when one of its two gates trips: the swap is too small to be worth making (the dust gate), or its gas would cost too large a share of its value (the gas gate). Then the compound deposits only the side that fits the current ratio and leaves the rest as a wallet residual, to be folded back in on the next rebalance (manual or automatic). Those fees still count: the Current panel shows them on its Fees Left Over line, and Profit and Net Profit and Loss Return add them. When the swap fires, the deposit is given the wallet’s whole post-swap balance of both tokens, so residuals left by earlier rebalances or compounds are swept in too, as far as they fit the current ratio. Coins swept in this way are not fees, and Fees Compounded does not count them. Initial Deposit for This LP counts them instead, each at the prices of the compound that took them in, so the Current panel’s Net Profit and Loss Return does not read them as a gain. What does not fit is cleared by the next rebalance, which always sweeps in every wallet residual for the pool when it mints the new position.
  • Why does a compound sometimes reinvest more than it collected? This is correct behavior, not a bug. When the ratio-correcting swap fires during a compound, the compounder deposits the wallet’s whole post-swap balance — which includes coins already sitting in the wallet: residuals from prior rebalances or compounds, or a deposit you made. So a log line like collected $5.14 reinvested $6.65 (fees $5.14, other coins from the wallet $1.51) residual $0.00 means the bot put $6.65 of value into the position: the newly collected $5.14 in fees, and $1.51 of coins that fit the current ratio range. The Telegram message names both: Compounded $5.14 in fees, and put $1.51 of coins the wallet already held into the position. Only the fees count as Fees Compounded. The other coins are not earnings: a deposit you made counts as money put in, and leftovers were already counted as the wallet residual. Old residuals getting put back to work is a feature — it cleans up dead tokens from prior moves without waiting for the next out-of-range or residual-cleanup rebalance.
  • Native Gas Token Wraps: EVM liquidity pools only accept the wrapped form of the native gas token (for example, $wPLS on PulseChain — the wrapped equivalent of native $PLS). LP Ranger detects each wrap as fresh capital by scanning the wrapped-token contract’s on-chain Deposit event, which records the exact wrapped amount minted to the wallet. The Deposit event is emitted whether the wrap is a plain wallet action or bundled inside a swap or rebalance, and whether or not the gas fee is netted from the wrap output, so wraps are credited at full precision with no tolerance fudge. Native $PLS sent to the wallet without being wrapped is not a deposit: no pool holds native $PLS, so LP Ranger has no transfer of a pool’s coin to read, and the $PLS stays in the wallet to pay for gas. Wrap it into $wPLS in the bot’s wallet, and the wrap counts as a $wPLS deposit, at $wPLS’s price. LP Ranger works only with the wrapped form of a native token. A deposit of the native token to a position, and any of it the position began with, count as $wPLS, at $wPLS’s price. LP Ranger’s own rebalances and compounds likewise put only the wallet’s $wPLS into a position, never its $PLS.
  • Need to Capture a Long Session of Server Logs? Start the app with the --log-file flag and every line that would scroll past in the terminal is also appended to a file that is located at <installation-root>/logs/lp-ranger.log. Usage: npm start -- --log-file (uses the default path above) or npm start -- --log-file path/to/run.log (custom path). The -- separator tells npm to forward the rest of the line to the app. For an always-on tee — useful on a long-lived headless host — copy the file app-config/app-defaults-for-user-configurable/logging.json to app-config/user-configurable/logging.json, edit your copy to set "enabled": true, and run npm start normally (no flag needed). Your copy in user-configurable/ is gitignored, so it survives tarball upgrades; the original in app-defaults-for-user-configurable/ must stay untouched. ANSI color escapes are stripped from the file so it’s grep-friendly; the terminal still shows colors. The file is opened in append mode — rotate or truncate it externally if it grows unbounded. To empty the file (free disk, start a clean capture, or scrub before sharing), run npm run clean:log. It empties the file where it is, so it works while the app is running.

Details

  • Gas Costs: The Current and Lifetime panels price gas at the current price of the native gas token (e.g. PLS on PulseChain), so their gas figures show what that gas is worth today. The Per-Day P&L table keeps each period’s gas at what it cost at the time, so the two will differ once the native token’s price has moved. A row whose day has no recorded price for the native token shows a dash for its gas, profit and net P&L, rather than a figure at today’s price.
  • Historical Prices: Historical token prices (from Moralis when a key is stored, then GeckoTerminal) value four things at the time they happened: each deposit, for Total Lifetime Deposit; the current NFT at its mint, its LP entry value; each closed period of the Per-Day P&L table; and the Historical figure of an Activity Log entry rebuilt from the blockchain. Impermanent loss/gain itself is measured at today’s prices. With a Moralis key stored, a past price is Moralis’s best figure at the block of the transaction it values. The free services generally offer only one price per day. So the same figure comes out different with a key and without one.

Edit Initial / Lifetime Deposit

LP Ranger adds up every deposit into the position — the first NFT’s mint and any coins added since, not the fees it reinvests — values each at the historical price of the block it was made in, and shows the total in the Lifetime panel as Total Lifetime Deposit. Coins you send to the wallet are counted as a deposit at the bot’s next check after they arrive, and again before each rebalance and compound. So Lifetime Net P&L and the Lifetime IL/G treat them as money you put in, not as a gain. Only coins someone else sends to the wallet count: from another wallet of yours, an exchange or a bridge. A transaction the bot’s wallet sends itself brings in no deposit. That keeps one position’s rebalances and compounds from counting as deposits in another. It also means coins you bring in by hand from the bot’s wallet, through a swap, a liquidity withdrawal, an ended stake, a claimed reward or a claimed bridge transfer, read as a gain. PLS you wrap into wPLS there still counts. The Current IL/G and the Impermanent Loss Guard count no coin in the wallet, so a deposit waiting there moves neither; once a rebalance or compound puts it into the position, they count it as put in. However, historical prices are not always available (especially for newer or low-liquidity tokens), so the app lets you enter the deposit value manually.

A coin two managed pools share. When the pools of two or more managed positions contain the same coin, such as two pools that both contain wPLS, a deposit of that coin is counted once in all, divided by where its coins go. While the coins wait in the wallet, each position counts as put in the same share of them that its Wallet Residual (Pool) line credits it: the amount of the coin its own NFT contains at the current price, out of the amount all those positions’ NFTs contain. When none of their NFTs contains any of the coin, the coin is divided by each position’s value in dollars instead. That share moves a little as prices move, and each position’s Total Lifetime Deposit moves with it. When one of those positions starts or stops, the others divide the coin again at once. A rebalance mints with the wallet’s whole balance, so the first of those positions to rebalance takes all of the deposit still waiting: from then on it counts all of it, and the others none. A compound takes part of it: of the wallet’s coins its deposit took in, the deposit’s share counts for that position, and the rest still waits.

How it works:

  • If you enter a value and save, your manual entry takes priority over the auto-detected value. The app will never overwrite your manual entry.
  • The auto-detected deposit from the blockchain scan runs independently in the background. It does not change your saved value — it only serves as a fallback when no manual value is set.
  • To revert to auto-detection, clear the input field (leave it empty) and click Save. This removes your manual override, and the app will use the auto-detected value on the next refresh.

A small info icon next to the deposit value tells you which source is being used: “Manually entered”, “Valued using Historical Price”, or “Valued using Current Price” (when historical prices were unavailable and the current price was used as a fallback).

Edit Total Lifetime Days

The Lifetime panel shows how many days the pool has been active for you, and that number drives every APR figure in the panel. Click Edit Total Lifetime Days to enter a value manually.

How it works:

  • Your manual entry wins outright. If you save a value, it overrides every auto-detected date. Type the number of days you want to see right now; tomorrow the display will read one higher, the day after that one higher again, and so on — you never need to re-edit as time passes.
  • To revert to auto-detection, clear the input (leave it empty) or save 0. The app will fall back to the auto-detected start date on the next refresh.
  • Auto-detection picks the mint date of the oldest NFT (for the current liquidity pool) on the current wallet.
  • If that NFT was originally minted on a different wallet and later transferred here, the app uses that NFT’s true mint block (one extra RPC lookup) instead of the arrival date, so time spent on the previous wallet still counts toward Lifetime Days. Only the single oldest NFT gets that lookup — the app does not spider into other wallets’ histories.
  • Edit manually whenever auto-detection is off — for example, if the NFT was minted more than 5 years ago (outside the on-chain scan window) or spent time on multiple prior wallets that the app does not spider.

Extras

LP Ranger ships with some optional extra capabilities, such as per-transaction bookkeeping.

Bookkeeping

Bookkeeping in coins means recording each transaction behind a position by the coins it moved and the gas it paid. LP Ranger ships with a set of utilities to assist with this bookkeeping, so that a user can get a per-transaction csv file summarizing their activity on their LP Ranger-managed liquidity positions. The set is comprised of two command-line tools: one reads a single transaction, and one writes a csv of a wallet’s rebalances and compounds over a range of days, with each amount’s value in USD on its day. For details, see the tools’ own user manual, docs/util/bookkeeping-for-coins-in-transactions/user-manual.md.
